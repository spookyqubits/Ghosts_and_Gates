College lab questions

In the spin lab, hold α at 90° and compare repeated estimates with 10, 100, and 1,000 shots. Then set α to 180°, try β = 0° and β = 90°, and explain why the exact energy changes from −1 to −3.

In the duel, record which public readings influenced your claims. Compare your best sampled setting with the setting that would actually have won. Explain why the game scores with exact energy.

Model and experiment

One spin uses H = Z. For two spins the Hamiltonian is H = XX + YY + ZZ. Prepare the first spin with Ry(β), apply a controlled-NOT from the first to the second spin, then rotate the first spin about Y by α. The expectations are ⟨XX⟩ = sin β cos α, ⟨YY⟩ = −sin β, and ⟨ZZ⟩ = cos α. Each Pauli term is sampled separately with outcomes ±1.

The singlet has exact energy −3. A product preparation has minimum −1 for this model. Summing three sampled Pauli means stays in [−3,+3]. A noisy estimate can reach −3 even when the exact energy at that setting is higher. The duel offsets both angle dials and samples 24 shots per term. The Scout sometimes plays at random. The Model opponent estimates the offsets from the public log.
