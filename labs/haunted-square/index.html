Haunted Square

Build a classical parity strategy and compare it with an ideal entangled team.

Alice and Bob agree on a plan, then go to separate rooms. They cannot communicate once the referee gives Alice a row and Bob a column. Their answers must follow the product rules, and they win a round if the signs at the intersection match.

Make a classical plan

Change the first two signs in each Alice row or Bob column. The remaining sign follows the product rule. Try to agree at as many of the nine intersections as possible.

Try the ideal entangled strategy

Choose the row Alice receives and the column Bob receives. The simulator draws their answers from the joint distribution for two shared Bell pairs. The players still cannot send messages. Each local answer has a random distribution that does not depend on the other player’s question.

College lab questions

Multiply all of Alice’s signs, grouping by row. Then do the same for Bob, grouping by column. If their plans agreed in every cell, the two totals would be the same. Use the product rules to explain why at least one cell has to disagree.

Try for eight matches out of nine. Then choose practice questions and compare the difference between covering every possible question and getting a lucky streak on a few questions.

Model and experiment

Alice answers a row and Bob answers a column. In the ideal model, the browser chooses uniformly from the eight pairs of answer triples that satisfy both players’ product rules and agree at the shared cell. Each player’s own answers have the same distribution whichever question the other person gets.

The players share two Bell pairs in the quantum model. The commuting Pauli observables are IX, XI, XX in the first row, ZI, IZ, ZZ in the second, and ZX, XZ, YY in the third. The browser calculates both answers for you.
