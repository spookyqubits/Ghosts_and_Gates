Test radioactive decay

  1. Open the lab, click the green flag, and choose how many half-lives to wait.
  2. Run one trial. If the simulated nucleus decays, the ideal detector records a click and triggers the outcome for the cat.
  3. Run 100 trials at each of 1, 2, and 3 half-lives. Compare your measured percentages with the predicted 50%, 75%, and 87.5%.

After t half-lives, the probability of decay is 1 − 2−t. The lab checks at whole half-life intervals. Decay model

The lab tests decay statistics. Schrödinger’s thought experiment asks how a quantum event leads to a definite outcome at the scale of a cat. Original thought experiment

Make a project of your own

  1. Open the editor on a computer and select Add Extension → Ghost Blocks.
  2. Try creating a ghost coin, applying ghost (the Hadamard gate), and checking the chance of measuring 1.
  3. Choose File → Save to your computer and share the project file and this editor link.

Try the quantum gates and measurement blocks

Start with a new ghost coin. Apply ghost (H), phase by 180°, then ghost again. The chance of measuring 1 rises to 100%. Try 90° instead and see how the odds change.

  • Phase (P) turns the amplitude of the 1 component by an angle. Use 90° for S or 45° for T.
  • Rotate turns a qubit around the X, Y, or Z axis of the Bloch sphere. Rotate a fresh coin 90° around Y and compare its odds in the X and Z bases.
  • Controlled Z changes the sign of the component where both qubits are 1. SWAP exchanges two qubits’ states. CCX flips the target when both controls are 1.
  • Chance % in a chosen basis shows the odds and leaves the state intact. Measure samples an outcome and updates the state. In the X and Y bases, 0 labels the +1 eigenstate and 1 labels the −1 eigenstate.
