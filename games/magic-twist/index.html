College lab questions

Turn off the spacing guide. Try 6°, 4°, 3°, and 2°. For each angle, use the 2 cm scale bar to estimate the gap between repeating centers. Turn the guide back on to check your estimate. Plot spacing against 1 / angle. What happens to the gap when you halve the angle?

Now try angles near 1.10°. How many dot spacings fit between neighboring centers? Compare 1.00°, 1.10°, and 1.20°. Why does changing the angle by a tenth of a degree matter more around 1.10° than around 6°?

Keep a table and a plot. Use them to explain what you can learn from the dot overlay and what you would need to measure in an actual graphene sample.

Model and graphene experiment

Try a smaller twist. The dots stay just as far apart within each layer, but the large clusters get farther apart. That larger repeating pattern is called a moiré.

For identical lattices with dot spacing a and twist angle θ, the moiré spacing is L = a / [2 sin(θ/2)]. At small angles it’s close to a/θ, with θ in radians. At 1.10°, that works out to about 52 dot spacings, or 10.42 cm in the tabletop model.

Using graphene’s 0.246 nm lattice spacing gives about 12.8 nm at the same angle. The overlay shows where the atomic patterns line up. In the 2018 experiment, researchers measured zero electrical resistance under certain conditions at low temperatures. Understanding that result takes measurements and a quantum model of how electrons behave in the material.
